# Primary Results

## Stratton Surges; Pritzker Wins the Night

The Illinois primary results are in, and **Lt. Gov. Juliana Stratton** pulled off a decisive victory in the Democratic U.S. Senate primary, defeating Rep. **Raja Krishnamoorthi** (~33.4%) and Rep. **Robin Kelly** (~18.4%) with roughly 39.7% of the vote. Krishnamoorthi conceded election night, despite having outspent the field by a wide margin — his campaign burned through roughly $29 million in ads.

The win is widely read as a validation of Gov. Pritzker's political operation, which poured resources into Stratton's campaign through a super PAC. Stratton heads into the general election as a heavy favorite against Republican nominee **Don Tracy** and, if she wins in November, would become the sixth Black woman ever elected to the U.S. Senate.

On the Republican side, **Darren Bailey** captured the gubernatorial nomination, setting up a Pritzker rematch — though Bailey lost to Pritzker by 13 points in 2022, and the political landscape hasn't shifted meaningfully in his favor since.

Other notable results:
- **9th Congressional District** (Schakowsky seat): Evanston Mayor **Daniel Biss** won a crowded 15-candidate Democratic primary.
- **2nd Congressional District** (Kelly's seat): Cook County Commissioner **Donna Miller** won the Democratic primary.
- **8th Congressional District** (Krishnamoorthi's seat): Former Rep. **Melissa Bean** won the Democratic primary.
- **Cook County Assessor**: **Pat Hynes** won the Democratic primary.

For _Claude Times_ readers: the newly elected state legislators will vote on [HB 2454](https://ilga.gov/Legislation/BillStatus?DocNum=2454&GAID=18&DocTypeID=HB&LegId=160217&SessionID=114) (cyclist safety), [HB 5626](https://ilga.gov/Legislation/BillStatus?DocNum=5626&GAID=18&DocTypeID=HB&SessionID=114) (the BUILD housing plan), and future state lead remediation funding. Watch for the legislative makeup of the new House and Senate as certified results come in.

# City Council

## Tip Credit Showdown — Results Pending

The [Chicago City Council](https://chicago.legistar.com/Calendar.aspx) met this morning at 10 a.m. with two high-stakes votes on the agenda.

**Ordinance O2025-0017549** — pulled onto the floor via a Rule 41 discharge motion by Ald. Samantha Nugent (39th) and Ald. Bennett Lawson (44th) — would **freeze Chicago's subminimum wage for tipped workers at its current 24% level**, halting the scheduled phase-down to 16% on July 1, 2026 under the 2023 One Fair Wage ordinance. Proponents claimed 26 votes; Mayor Johnson called the freeze "tone deaf" and signaled a possible veto, which would require 34 votes to override. Results from today's vote are expected to be reported shortly by the [Chicago Tribune](https://www.chicagotribune.com) and [Chicago Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/city-hall).

**Ordinance O2025-002004** — which would formally authorize COPA (the Civilian Office of Police Accountability) to investigate CPD officers for alleged Welcoming City Ordinance violations — was also up for a vote after being blocked by a procedural maneuver at February's meeting. The ordinance advanced 19-3 through a joint committee in January.

Also on the agenda: a **$27 million settlement** for the family of Stacy Vaughn-Harrell, killed when a driver being chased by CPD crashed into her vehicle in 2017.

# Bikes

## Brighton Park Pushes Back on Archer Ave

The most timely bike story this week isn't on the North Side. Brighton Park residents and business owners are publicly opposing the **recently installed concrete barriers on Archer Avenue**, which reduced the roadway from four lanes to two as part of a Complete Streets redesign.

Business owner Juan Castro told [The Center Square](https://www.thecentersquare.com/illinois/article_81e5e3f2-8b55-4e2f-a45d-3a5dda7fa44d.html) the project is making people "not want to stay in the neighborhood" — and alleged that emergency vehicles have been impeded. "Un desastre!" summed up the sentiment from several quoted residents. CDOT maintains that Complete Streets improvements benefit safety for everyone.

The Archer Ave pushback rhymes with the ongoing **Grand Avenue Phase 2** drama in West Town, where the Fulton Market Association and affiliated business coalition is seeking to replace the planned protected bike lanes (Damen to Ogden) with shared bus-bike lanes. Better Streets Chicago has called the compromise "not in good faith." CDOT is holding firm on Phase 2, which remains scheduled for 2026 with no contractor announcement yet.

The **Portage Park Neighborhood Bike Network** survey has now closed with over 500 responses, following the packed March 5 community meeting (200+ attendees, line into the parking lot). CDOT will hold additional meetings before any installation decision; the target window is late 2026 or early 2027.

# Lead

## Federal Funding Under Pressure

No breaking news from the past 24 hours, but the federal context continues to tighten. Congress has been weighing cuts to the final tranche of the federal lead pipe replacement program, with safe water advocates pushing back. Illinois received the largest state allocation of EPA lead pipe funds in November 2025 — but Chicago still needs to roughly triple its current replacement pace (~8,300/year) to meet the EPA's Lead and Copper Rule Improvements deadline, which requires ~20,000 replacements/year starting in 2027. The city's $325M federal WIFIA loan remains substantially undrawn.

# Housing & Abundance

## ADU Countdown: 14 Days

The citywide ADU ordinance takes effect **April 1** — two weeks from today. Starting then, ADUs are permitted as-of-right in all RT and RM multifamily zoning districts citywide (excluding the downtown core). The 46-0 Council vote from September 2025 was decisive; the main caveats are a union apprentice labor requirement (which narrows the contractor pool) and aldermanic opt-in still required for single-family RS zones.

[Chicago Cityscape's ADU tracker](https://www.chicagocityscape.com/adu/) and the [city's ADU page](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/sites/additional-dwelling-units-ordinance/home.html) have the details.

**BUILD plan (HB 5626)** remains in the spring session without a floor vote before April. Today's primary results — and the composition of the incoming state legislature — will shape whether the votes are there when it comes up.
