# Housing & Abundance

## ADU Day Has Arrived

After nearly 70 years in which attic apartments and coach houses were largely illegal to build in Chicago, the city's permanent ADU ordinance is effective as of today. The application portal at the Chicago Department of Housing opened this morning, accepting permit applications citywide for the first time.

The numbers are significant. The number of parcels eligible for ADU construction jumped from roughly **115,000 to 245,000** — a 135% expansion over the pilot zones. Any property in a multifamily (RT or RM) zoning district outside the downtown core can now add a conversion unit or coach house as-of-right. Single-family (RS) zones remain an aldermanic opt-in patchwork, with **34 wards** having opted in some or all of their RS areas since September.

A few practical notes for anyone considering applying:

- Pre-certification applications (paused since March 18) resume today through the Department of Housing. DOH pre-certification is a prerequisite, but does not replace the Department of Buildings permit — that's a separate process.
- Coach houses come with an apprenticeship mandate: any contractor must be enrolled in a federally recognized labor apprenticeship program. No other housing construction in Chicago carries this requirement.
- Properties adding two or more ADUs must make at least 50% affordable at 60% AMI.
- Processing times have been running roughly 120 days.

Also launching today: **ChiBlockBuilder's largest-ever inventory of city-owned lots** — more than 600 parcels in 25 wards, collectively valued at $34 million, available for housing, mixed use, side yards, urban agriculture, and open space. The lots range from about $5,000 to $1 million. An informational webinar for market-rate buyers is scheduled for **Tuesday, April 7 at 1 p.m.** A Missing Middle webinar follows on **April 13**.

# Bikes & Divvy

## Divvy Announces Major 2026 Expansion

Mayor Johnson, the Chicago Department of Transportation, and Lyft announced this week that Divvy will add more than **200 new or upgraded stations in 2026**, building on last year's 140-station expansion. Ridership is already outpacing prior winter records: the system has logged more than **500,000 trips so far in 2026**, following a record 6.8 million trips in 2025. Combined with Lime scooters, Chicago saw nearly 13 million shared micromobility trips last year.

On pricing: the **$99 annual membership** for new and lapsed riders continues, while current member rates are frozen at $143 for a third consecutive year. Unlock fees are waived for trips beginning in Equity Priority Areas. Enrollment in the **Divvy for Everyone** program — $5 annual memberships for income-eligible residents — is being streamlined this year.

CDOT also ticked off another milestone: the city has now installed over **100 miles of new bikeways** since the current administration took office, against a stated goal of 500 miles. Mayor Johnson held a press conference this week to mark the 100-mile mark. Whether 500 is achievable before the 2027 mayoral election is a reasonable question, but the momentum in 2025-2026 is real.

One note of sobriety: a separate analysis found bicycle crashes have risen sharply — nearly 50% between 2022 and 2025 — as ridership climbs. More bikes on more streets, some of them protected lanes and some not, means more exposure. The infrastructure buildout and the safety record need to close that gap.

# City Council

## Tipped Wage: Countdown to April 15

No movement since the March 25 veto. The math still disfavors the override coalition — they had 30 votes on March 18, need 34 on April 15. No alderperson has publicly signaled a switch. Mayor Johnson appears likely to hold his legislative win intact at the local level.

The more unpredictable front remains Springfield. Rep. Curtis Tarver's **HB 4623** — which would strip home rule authority on tipped wages and effectively drop Chicago's tipped wage floor back to the state rate of $9/hour — has passed out of committee 22-4. The full legislative timeline is unclear, but it is alive. If both the City Council override fails and HB 4623 dies in the Senate, tipped workers see their next raise on July 1 (to 84% of the full $16.60 minimum wage). If HB 4623 passes and is signed by Pritzker, all of that unravels.

# Vegan Food

## A Shakeout, Not a Collapse

The closures have been hard to miss. In the span of eight days in late November, Chicago lost Kitchen 17 (Avondale), Native Foods (Loop), Chicago Raw (Streeterville), and The Chicago Diner's Logan Square location. Bloom Plant Based Kitchen — recently named one of the city's best — followed in February, citing Valentine's Day as its farewell service.

What's happening? The picture is more complex than a simple vegan backlash. Post-pandemic office vacancies decimated lunch-dependent spots (Native Foods depended heavily on Loop foot traffic). Millennial meat consumption, per market research firm Datassential, rose 17% between 2021 and 2024, while Millennial veganism dipped 4%. Rising labor, food, and rent costs are punishing lower-margin operations across the board. And ICE raids, which began this year, have contributed to reduced restaurant foot traffic citywide.

The silver lining: Chicago still has roughly **30 fully vegan restaurants** according to HappyCow, and Bloom's team has signaled they may reopen under a different concept. The category may be consolidating toward places with a distinct community identity or a hybrid model — rather than shrinking to zero.

Worth noting: Bloom's closure was covered in our February issue. We haven't confirmed a new opening of note this week; if you've spotted one, let us know.

# Wildlife

## Rat Contraceptive Update

Covered yesterday but worth a brief update: the Wicker Park/Bucktown pilot's published data (from SenesTech's February press release) showed an **88% reduction in track density** and a **79% reduction in overall plate presence** from August 2025 through January 2026. SSA No. 33 plans to expand the program further into Wicker Park and Bucktown later this year.

The Lincoln Park pilot — run by the 43rd Ward, Chicago Bird Alliance, Lincoln Park Zoo, and Lincoln Park Conservancy — began in August 2025. Comparative data from the four treated vs. four control blocks is expected in **summer 2026**.

# Farm Bill

## Post-Easter Floor Vote

Congress returns from Easter recess around April 7. Chair GT Thompson has confirmed the House floor vote on **H.R. 7567** is now expected in that week — the pre-Easter target having slipped. The political friction hasn't resolved: MAHA-aligned Republicans remain skeptical of the pesticide liability shield in Section 10205; Democrats are largely opposed over SNAP provisions. Floor passage in the House is uncertain, and Senate action remains further off still.
