# Local

## "No Kings" Day Fills Grant Park and the Loop

Yesterday's national "No Kings" day of action drew a massive crowd to Butler Field in Grant Park, with organizers claiming approximately 200,000 Chicagoans. The march moved south on Columbus Drive, west on Ida B. Wells Drive, north on Dearborn toward Trump Tower, then east across Wacker and back down Michigan Avenue, concluding peacefully around 5 p.m. Mayor Brandon Johnson, Sen. Dick Durbin, Lt. Gov. Juliana Stratton, and Rep. Chuy Garcia addressed the crowd. Nationally, organizers reported 8 million participants at more than 3,300 events worldwide.

# Wildlife

## Colorado SB26-062: Tomorrow Is the House Test

Colorado's **SB26-062** — the bill restricting SGARs (second-generation anticoagulant rodenticides), first-generation anticoagulants, and glue traps — faces its **House Agriculture, Water & Natural Resources Committee hearing tomorrow, Monday March 30, at 1:30 PM** in HCR 0107. The bill passed the full Senate 22–12 on March 18, but enters a House chamber that has historically been less receptive to animal welfare legislation than the Senate, and agricultural opposition has been fierce throughout.

Colorado residents can still submit written testimony at the bill's [official page](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB26-062). If the bill survives committee, it heads to the House floor in a chamber that will be the harder of the two.

## Farm Bill: The Easter Window Has Closed

House Agriculture Chair GT Thompson's goal of a full House floor vote on **H.R. 7567** before Easter has gone unmet: the Easter recess began March 28 with no scheduled vote. The bill cleared committee 34–17 on March 5 but has stalled in the Rules Committee pipeline, caught between MAHA-aligned Republicans objecting to the pesticide liability shield provisions (Sections 10205–10207) and House Democratic opposition. Members return after Easter; when the bill comes back, the 135-member Democratic opposition letter and a coalition of 300+ doctors, farmers, and MAHA leaders will still be waiting.

# Bikes & Transit

## CTA Funding: TRO Deadline Passes Without Appeal

The March 27 deadline Judge Thomas Durkin set for the Trump DOT to begin releasing CTA funds passed without a publicly reported 7th Circuit appeal or emergency stay. Per the Mass Transit reporting updated through March 26, the ruling "allows CTA to resume drawing down federal reimbursements" on both the **Red Line Extension** and **Red & Purple Modernization** projects — averting the demobilization threat that had been looming over both. No DOT compliance confirmation has been published, but construction on both projects is continuing. The underlying lawsuit proceeds; this is a temporary restraining order, not a final judgment, and the administration's prior playbook in analogous cases (the Hudson Tunnel project in New York) suggests more legal skirmishing ahead.

## Violet Harris: Eight Days, No Arrest

It has now been eight days since 15-year-old **Violet Harris** was killed by a wrong-way hit-and-run driver while riding a Lime e-scooter at 77th and Exchange in South Shore. No arrest, no public suspect description. CPD's Major Accident Detectives continue to investigate; the incident was captured on a Police Observation Device camera. Harris is the sixth vulnerable road user killed on Chicago streets in 2026.

Separately, Streetsblog Chicago published a pointed critique last week of the Chicago Tribune's initial coverage, which centered e-scooter risks rather than driver accountability and road infrastructure failures. The framing debate is not academic: how Chicago's media and institutions assign responsibility in cases like this shapes whether the city invests in protected infrastructure or lectures teenagers about vehicle choice.

# Abundance

## ADU Portal: Three Days Out

The Chicago ADU permit portal opens **Wednesday, April 1**, when the permanent citywide ordinance takes effect. Starting that day, applications for coach houses (detached ADUs) and conversion units (interior) can be submitted across all RT/RM zoning districts citywide. Single-family (RS) zones remain aldermanic opt-in only, with 28 wards currently enrolled. Average permit processing time runs about 121 days — a clock that starts only after you apply.
