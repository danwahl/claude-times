# Bikes & Road Safety

## Hyde Park Academy Has Buried Two Students in Two Weeks

The hit-and-run epidemic has a new face — two of them, in fact.

**Violet Harris**, 15, a freshman at Hyde Park Academy, was struck and killed while riding a Lime e-scooter on March 21 at 77th and Exchange in South Shore. **Lanai Smith**, 18, a senior at the same school, was struck and killed on March 30 at Sibley Boulevard and Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Drive in Dolton while switching buses on her way to her restaurant job — just days before graduation, with nursing school lined up. Nine days apart. Same school. Zero arrests in either case.

CPD's Major Accident Detectives have identified a suspected vehicle in Smith's death — a 2007 red Chevrolet Corvette, Illinois plate FR 56003 — but the driver has not been located. Harris's case remains open with no identified vehicle. Both drivers fled; both remain at large.

The pairing is brutal in a way that statistics rarely are. South Shore logged 83 hit-and-runs in the first three months of 2026, one in three drivers fleeing after a crash on the neighborhood's main corridors. But when two students from the same school on the same South Side die nine days apart in the same manner, the pattern stops being a statistic and becomes a neighborhood's grief.

Meanwhile, on April 3, a 16-year-old driver was charged in a crash on the Southwest Side that killed a 15-year-old girl and injured two others at Marquette Park — a case of the system actually working. The contrast is clarifying: accountability doesn't require a political will to appear, just a driver who didn't flee.

If you have information on the Dolton case (red Corvette, IL FR 56003), contact CPD Major Accidents at 312-745-4521.

# Wildlife

## Lincoln Park's Rat Contraceptive Pilot: No Poison, Some Peanut Butter

The Chicago Bird Alliance, Lincoln Park Zoo's Urban Wildlife Institute, and the 43rd Ward office are six months into a pilot project that replaces rodenticide bait boxes with contraceptive ones. The active ingredient is an extract of **thunder god vine**, an Asian plant long used in traditional Chinese medicine for inflammatory conditions — with a notable side effect: it interrupts both sperm production and egg release in rodents.

The program, launched in August 2025 after the high-profile deaths of Lincoln Park's great horned owls from anticoagulant rodenticide exposure, uses peanut butter palettes inside alley bait boxes. Rats need to consume it every 30 to 60 days to remain infertile. It's not hormonal, and early results have not surfaced the collateral toxicity problems that plagued the area's previous poisoning approach.

Initial data is expected in **summer 2026**. Wicker Park and Bucktown are running a parallel program via the local chamber of commerce.

The approach won't fully displace lethal control — the city's rodent-baiting program continues elsewhere — but the pilots are building the evidence base that rodenticide-free management can work in dense urban settings. For those following the Colorado SB26-062 bill (still awaiting a House committee vote, with an April 6 meeting on the calendar), the Lincoln Park and Wicker Park pilots represent precisely the kind of alternative-model precedent that advocates cite when making the case that restrictions on SGARs are viable.

# Abundance

## ADU Era Officially Begins: 60% of the City, Now Citywide

Chicago's Accessory Dwelling Unit ordinance went **permanent and citywide on April 1**, ending the three-year pilot that had limited coach houses and garden apartments to roughly 20% of residential and mixed-use areas in selected wards. As of this week, about **60% of Chicago's residential and mixed-use zones** can now build an ADU by right — no special variance, no pilot-ward lottery.

The expansion is the result of the ADU pilot program that debuted in 2021 in five wards, demonstrated strong demand (over 30 applications in the first 12 hours of the April 1 launch), and built a coalition that could hold together a council supermajority. Ald. Bennett Lawson's office was central to the final push.

Separately, Mayor Johnson announced a **$300 million affordable housing investment** spread across 15 projects creating or preserving 1,223 rental units citywide — a reminder that market-rate ADU production and subsidized affordable housing aren't substitutes for each other. Both are needed; neither alone closes the gap.
