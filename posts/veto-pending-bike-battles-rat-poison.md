# Tip Credit Veto: Still en Route

As of this morning, Mayor Johnson has not yet formally transmitted his veto of the tip credit freeze — the ordinance that passed 30-18 on Wednesday, halting One Fair Wage's phase-in schedule. The formal veto is expected any day. Johnson has called it "the only fair thing to do" and framed it explicitly as a racial justice issue, noting that 60% of tipped workers are Black or Latina women. With only 30 votes — four short of the 34 needed to override — the veto is expected to hold, meaning the One Fair Wage phase-in toward full wage parity by 2028 resumes as scheduled.

Nothing to do but watch.

# Bikes

## Two Fronts of Backlash

The past week has surfaced two distinct waves of anti-bike-lane organizing, worth tracking as signals of the political headwinds CDOT faces:

**Brighton Park / Archer corridor**: Residents and business owners along Archer Ave, where Phase 1 protected lanes and concrete barriers have already been installed, have been vocal about disruption. A [Center Square report from March 16](https://www.thecentersquare.com/illinois/article_81e5e3f2-8b55-4e2f-a45d-3a5dda7fa44d.html) quotes one business owner calling it "un desastre." This is a familiar pattern — backlash tends to peak immediately after construction, before new traffic patterns settle in — but in an aldermanic system, "familiar" doesn't mean "harmless."

**Grand Avenue coalition**: A business coalition in West Town has formalized its opposition to Grand Ave Phase 2 (Damen to Ogden), arguing the lane conversion will harm deliveries and customer access. [CBS Chicago covered the coalition's launch this week.](https://www.cbsnews.com/chicago/news/coalition-opposes-bike-lane-construction-grand-avenue-west-town-downtown/) CDOT has been meeting with stakeholders but has not announced any design changes. Grand Ave Phase 2 is currently on the city's 2026 project list — but so was Grand Ave Phase 1, for a while.

**The bigger picture**: A [Tribune op-ed from cycling advocates (March 6)](https://www.chicagotribune.com/2026/03/06/opinion-bike-lanes-chicago-brandon-johnson-against/) charges that Johnson has "talked the talk" on bikes — he received the Active Transportation Alliance's endorsement in 2023 — but is overseeing a capital program that slashed Complete Streets and Vision Zero funding by 70% and projects only 23 new miles of bikeway in 2026 (vs. 50+ miles in 2023 and 2024). The op-ed calls for Johnson to personally intervene on the 2027 capital budget before the window closes.

# Wildlife

## Australia Bans the Bad Rat Poison. Can Illinois Follow?

On March 10, Australia officially banned consumer sales of **second-generation anticoagulant rodenticides** (SGARs) — products like brodifacoum and bromadiolone that kill not just rodents but the hawks, owls, coyotes, and foxes that eat them. [The Conversation has the details.](https://theconversation.com/new-ban-on-dangerous-rodent-poisons-is-lifeline-for-our-native-animals-278072) Australia's move follows similar restrictions in parts of Canada; Connecticut classified SGARs as restricted-use as of January 2026; Rhode Island has a pending ban bill.

Illinois does not.

Chicago sits at a major raptor migration corridor, and the [Chicago Bird Alliance](https://chicagobirdalliance.org/issues-advocacy-all/2024/8/9/rodenticides-and-birds) has been tracking SGAR-linked raptor deaths for years. The city's pilot of CO2 traps (kill-but-don't-poison) is ongoing, and a rat oral contraceptive trial remains pending city approval. But SGARs remain freely available at hardware stores across the state.

The Illinois General Assembly is in session through May — and there's currently no SGAR bill filed in Springfield. That's an open lane.

# Housing & Abundance

## ADU Permit Day: 12 Days Out

The permanent ADU ordinance takes effect **April 1** — and permit applications open the same day. Quick orientation for the uninitiated: ADUs (coach houses, basement apartments, garage conversions) are now permitted as-of-right in all multifamily-zoned areas city-wide. Alderpersons retain opt-in authority for single-family zones outside the original pilot areas. No added parking is required. Two or more ADUs per building triggers affordability requirements (50% at 60% AMI). Coach house construction must use union apprentice labor.

[Chicago Cityscape's ADU tracker](https://www.chicagocityscape.com/adu/) will log permit activity as it comes in — a useful gauge of how much pent-up demand actually materializes.

## BUILD Plan: Springfield on Hold

HB 5626 — Gov. Pritzker's "missing middle" statewide housing bill — remains in the House Rules Committee with no floor vote scheduled before April. The bill would require all Illinois municipalities to allow up to 8-unit buildings by right in any single-family zone, mandate ADU allowances statewide, cap parking minimums, and open the door to private third-party building inspectors. The Metropolitan Mayors Caucus is calling for a stakeholder process before a vote. Chicago is effectively neutral (its zoning already exceeds most of the bill's density floors). The bill's real teeth are aimed at the suburbs. Session runs through May.
