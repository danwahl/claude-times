# City Council

## Veto Signed; Override Clock Starts

Mayor Johnson signed his veto of the Council's tipped wage freeze on **March 25**, transmitting it formally back to City Council — his third veto of his term. The override math remains heavily against the freeze's backers: they had 30 votes on March 18, well short of the 34 needed. No alderpersons have publicly signaled they'd flip.

The next scheduled City Council meeting is **April 15**, which is when an override vote could formally occur. That's the Illinois Restaurant Association's lobbying window.

Meanwhile, a parallel bill in Springfield bears watching: State Rep. Curtis Tarver's bill to preempt local tipped wage regulation passed out of committee 22–4. If that bill advances and is signed, it could unwind the One Fair Wage ordinance at the state level regardless of what happens with the veto.

# Lead

## Replacing Lead Pipes Could Create 90,000 Illinois Jobs

A report released **March 23** makes a striking economic argument for lead pipe replacement: doing it statewide could create up to **90,000 jobs over a decade**. The framing is deliberate. Advocates are pitching lead remediation as workforce development at a moment when federal funding faces real cuts.

Illinois has the most lead service lines in the country — roughly 1.5 million, with Chicago accounting for about 412,000 of them (nearly 30% of the statewide total). The city is currently replacing ~8,300 lines per year, a pace that won't finish the job until **2076** — 30 years past the EPA's 2047 deadline.

Trump administration budget proposals would slash the low-interest loan program for lead pipe work by more than half. Illinois lawmakers led by Rep. Raja Krishnamoorthi have pressed the EPA to release $3 billion in stalled Infrastructure Law funding, warning that delays amount to "dangerous politicization." The jobs framing adds a new dimension to that advocacy: even members skeptical of public health spending might warm to the employment angle, particularly in a tight budget environment.

# Wildlife

## Colorado SB26-062 Hearing Confirmed; Farm Bill Still in the Queue

**Colorado SB26-062** — the bill that would restrict SGAR rodenticide use to licensed professional applicators — has its House Agriculture, Water & Natural Resources Committee hearing confirmed for **Monday, March 30 at 1:30 PM** in HCR 0107. The bill passed the full Senate 22–12 and arrived in the House on March 19. Monday is the next gauntlet.

Colorado advocates wishing to submit written testimony or attend in person: [House Agriculture, Water & Natural Resources Committee page](https://leg.colorado.gov/committees/2026A/house/AgricultureWaterNaturalResources).

On the federal front: no House floor vote has occurred on **H.R. 7567** (the Farm, Food, and National Security Act, with its pesticide preemption Sections 10205–10206). Chair Thompson is still targeting a pre-Easter floor vote — before April 5. One new wrinkle worth watching: the MAHA (Make America Healthy Again) wing of House Republicans has become vocally critical of the pesticide liability shield in Section 10205, creating an unusual cross-partisan fault line. Whether that translates to actual floor defections is uncertain, but it's a fracture that didn't exist a week ago.

The action window is real and short: if you want to weigh in with your House member before a floor vote, now is the time.

# Housing & Abundance

## Springfield Takes Aim at Zoning — The BUILD Package

While Chicago's local ordinance activity has been the focus here, the bigger housing supply news this week is in Springfield. Governor Pritzker's **BUILD** package — unveiled at the February 18 State of the State — is the most ambitious housing preemption push in Illinois in decades, and it's moving.

The centerpiece bill is **HB 5626** (Rep. Kam Buckner), currently pending in House Rules Committee. It would:
- Legalize **middle housing** (two-flats, triplexes) by right on lots over 2,500 sq ft, statewide
- Require **ADUs** to be allowed on all residential lots
- Eliminate exclusionary **parking minimums** statewide
- Preempt local bans on **single-stair residential buildings** up to six stories (effective January 1, 2027)
- Set floor-not-ceiling standards for setbacks, height, and lot coverage that localities can loosen but not tighten

Companion Senate bills: **SB 4061** (Sen. Feigenholtz) on single-stair reform; **SB 4060** on middle housing.

That single-stair preemption clause is notable for Chicago specifically: if HB 5626 passes, Ald. Martin's local single-stair ordinance becomes somewhat beside the point — the city would be legally required to allow single-stair builds regardless. The state would move faster than City Hall.

Opposition is organized. The Illinois Municipal League unveiled its "Moving Cities Forward" platform on March 4 — explicitly prioritizing local control over housing as a counter to preemption. This fight is going to be loud through the end of the spring session in May.

The urgency behind it: a University of Illinois study pegs Illinois's housing shortage at approximately **142,000 units**, requiring 227,000 new units over five years to close the gap.

## Six Days to ADU Day

The permanent ADU ordinance and the City's permit application portal open **April 1** — six days out. Pre-certification applications have been paused since March 18 and resume the same day.

Quick orientation for newcomers: the permanent ordinance allows ADUs by right in all multifamily (RT/RM) zones citywide — roughly 29% of the city's land area. Single-family (RS) zones remain aldermanic opt-in only; the city added four more wards in February (5th, 31st, 34th, 36th), bringing the total to 28. A meaningful expansion — but, as Steven Vance noted, not truly citywide.

## Missing Middle Keeps Drumbeat Going

The Missing Middle Infill Housing program continues to advance quietly. The latest: the city has proposed a **four-building, three-flat project in Morgan Park** through Far South Community Development Corp. — a $7.8M investment with up to $150K per unit in city support. This follows the Round 2 and Round 3 approvals in January and February; the program now has 300+ units advanced across 106 buildings, with 500+ total homes expected.
