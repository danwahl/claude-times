# Wildlife

## The Rat Pill Is Working in Wicker Park

Promising early results from Chicago's rat contraceptive pilot in Wicker Park: organizers say rat activity on the program's treated commercial corridors has measurably declined, and expansion is now planned to other parts of Wicker Park and Bucktown later this year.

The program, run by [Special Service Area No. 33](https://business.wickerparkbucktown.com/Residential-Rat-Birth-Control) in partnership with Arizona-based [SenesTech](https://www.evolverodentbirthcontrol.com/wickerpark), deploys a product called **Evolve** — a cottonseed-oil-based contraceptive rated a "minimum risk pesticide" by the EPA, meaning it poses little or no risk to people or the environment, including non-target animals. Bait boxes were installed along portions of North Avenue, Ashland Avenue, Milwaukee Avenue, and Division Street over the past year.

"We don't know exactly where [expansion] will be yet," said SSA No. 33's Howe, "but our footprint spans almost 14 miles throughout Wicker Park and Bucktown, and we're only right now covering a portion of that."

Meanwhile, the parallel **Lincoln Park WISDOM Good Bites™ pilot** — the city-affiliated study run by the Chicago Bird Alliance and Lincoln Park Zoo's Dr. Maureen Murray, started August 2025 — is entering its critical spring phase. Ald. Knudsen now says he expects preliminary data **later this spring** rather than waiting for the full summer report. The spring rat emergence is the real test: do the treated eight blocks show a meaningfully smaller population than the control blocks? If yes, the case for a citywide ordinance granting all 50 wards access to contraceptive programs becomes much harder to argue against.

## Colorado SB26-062: Monday Is the Next Gauntlet

The House Agriculture, Water & Natural Resources Committee hearing for **Colorado SB26-062** — the bill restricting SGARs, first-generation anticoagulant rodenticides, and glue traps — is scheduled for **Monday, March 30 at 1:30 PM** in HCR 0107 at the state capitol. The bill passed the full Senate 22–12 on March 18 after a Senate Appropriations detour, and was assigned to this House committee on March 19.

If the bill survives Monday, it heads toward a floor vote in a chamber that has historically been less favorable to animal welfare legislation than the Senate. Colorado residents can still submit written testimony at [leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB26-062](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB26-062).

# Lead

## Lead Pipe Replacement Could Generate 90,000 Illinois Jobs

A [new report](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/23032026/replacing-toxic-lead-pipes-could-drive-job-creation-in-illinois-report-finds/) published March 23 by four Illinois organizations — **Current, Elevate, HIRE360, and Metropolitan Planning Council** — reframes Chicago's lead pipe crisis as a potential economic engine. The case in brief:

- Illinois has the **most lead pipes in the country**: 667,000 confirmed plus 820,000 suspected lines, with Chicago alone accounting for nearly 30%.
- Fully replacing them would cost **$6–10 billion** statewide. Federal funding covers roughly $1 billion of that.
- Already-allocated federal funds, run through the workforce multipliers used by the EPA and the American Water Works Association, would generate roughly **2,000 direct jobs and 9,000 indirect jobs**. Close the full funding gap, and those figures rise to **35,000 direct and 55,000 indirect — 90,000 over a decade**.
- The current construction workforce isn't representative enough to meet this need: only 3.8% of registered Illinois construction apprentices are women; only 10% are Black. The report recommends building diversity and equity requirements directly into project contracts.

The argument is straightforward: Illinois has to replace these pipes regardless. The question is whether the state's capital investment strategy treats it as a public health emergency only, or also as an opportunity to build a more equitable skilled trades workforce in the communities most harmed by lead exposure.

# Bikes & Transit

## Federal Judge Orders DOT to Release $3 Billion for CTA

On March 24, U.S. District Judge Thomas Durkin granted the Chicago Transit Authority a temporary restraining order, ruling that the Trump administration must unfreeze over **$3 billion** in federal transit funds it had been withholding since October 2025. The frozen money covers the **Red Line Extension** (5.5 miles, four new Far South Side stations) and other major CTA capital projects.

The administration had claimed it was reviewing Chicago infrastructure grants for potential "race-based contracting" — but Durkin noted pointedly that DOT had applied this scrutiny only to major projects in **Chicago and New York**, suggesting the reviews are "a pretextual basis for some other interest unrelated to actual compliance." He found the CTA likely to prevail on four separate grounds, including arbitrary and capricious rulemaking, failure to follow APA notice requirements, and violations of federal grant management regulations.

The ruling is temporary pending a possible government appeal, and the administration's legal playbook — it's already fighting a similar ruling on New York's Hudson Tunnel project — suggests this isn't over. But construction on affected projects had been at risk of **demobilization**; the order stops that clock.

# Abundance

## ADU Portal: Four Days Out

The Chicago ADU permit portal opens **April 1** — now four days away — when the permanent ordinance takes effect. Starting that day, applications can be submitted for coach houses (detached ADUs) and conversion units (interior) across all multifamily RT/RM districts citywide, plus the original pilot zones. Single-family (RS) zones remain aldermanic opt-in only; 28 wards have joined.

Key reminder: ADUs cannot be used for short-term rentals (Airbnb etc.). Long-term leasing or family housing only. Average permit processing time is about 121 days, so the sooner you apply, the sooner you can build.

## Springfield Escalation: Tipped Wage Bill Clears Committee 22–4

A brief update on a story from yesterday: **HB 4623**, Rep. Curtis Tarver's bill to give the state exclusive authority over tipped wage rules (effectively stripping Chicago's home-rule power and reverting tipped workers to the state rate of approximately **$9/hour**), cleared the House Labor and Commerce Committee 22–4 on March 26. If it passes the full General Assembly and is signed, it would unwind the One Fair Wage ordinance regardless of what happens at April 15's City Council override vote. The IRA is pushing the Springfield angle hard; so far, Mayor Johnson and council allies are holding firm at City Hall.
