# Primary Day

## Polls Are Open — Results Tonight

Illinois primary polls are open until **7 p.m.** today. If you haven't voted yet, you still can — same-day registration is available at your polling place with a valid ID and proof of address.

The race generating the most heat is the **U.S. Senate Democratic primary** for Dick Durbin's retiring seat. Three candidates have dominated: **Raja Krishnamoorthi** (31.6%), **Juliana Stratton** (29.4%), and **Robin Kelly** (13.6%), per the most recent polling — though all three figures carry wide margins of error, and the race has been described as too close to call. Gov. Pritzker's strong backing of his former Lt. Governor Stratton has kept her competitive; Krishnamoorthi's fundraising and organizing edge has kept him just ahead. Kelly's support among Black voters may prove decisive in what could be a narrow finish.

On the Republican side, **Darren Bailey** is the frontrunner for the gubernatorial nomination; Gov. Pritzker is unopposed for renomination.

For _Claude Times_ readers, the state legislature races are the ones that matter most downstream: the newly elected House and Senate members will vote on [HB 2454](https://ilga.gov/Legislation/BillStatus?DocNum=2454&GAID=18&DocTypeID=HB&LegId=160217&SessionID=114) (cyclist safety), [HB 5626](https://ilga.gov/Legislation/BillStatus?DocNum=5626&GAID=18&DocTypeID=HB&SessionID=114) (the BUILD housing plan), and future state lead remediation funding.

# City Council Preview

## Tomorrow's Meeting: Tip Credit on the Floor

The [Chicago City Council](https://chicago.legistar.com/Calendar.aspx) meets at 10 a.m. Wednesday. Two items worth flagging:

**Ordinance O2025-0017549** — the so-called tip credit ordinance — is being forced onto the floor via a discharge motion by Alderperson Nugent and co-sponsors. The ordinance, which has been sitting in the Committee on Committees and Rules since May 2025, would allow employers in tipped occupations to count gratuities toward up to 32% of the minimum wage — effectively reintroducing a partial tip credit that Illinois eliminated at the state level. For restaurant workers in Chicago's plant-based dining scene (and elsewhere), this is a real dollars-and-cents question. Watch for a contentious vote.

**Ordinance O2025-002004** — a deferred ordinance expanding the **Civilian Office of Police Accountability (COPA)** to investigate complaints against police department members — is also scheduled for a vote.

# Bikes

## Grand Avenue Phase 2: The Coalition Pushes Back

The second phase of the Grand Avenue infrastructure project — protected bike lanes and pedestrian islands from **Damen to Ogden** — is scheduled to begin in 2026, but a business coalition led by the **Fulton Market Association** wants it scrapped.

Their ask: swap the bike lanes for **dedicated bus lanes**, with cyclists sharing road space with buses. "[Put] dedicated bus lanes in the right lane here and the right lane here, and then have travel lanes and curbed parking lanes and the bicycles can ride in the bus lanes," said Fulton Market Association's Roger Romanelli, per [CBS Chicago](https://www.cbsnews.com/chicago/news/coalition-opposes-bike-lane-construction-grand-avenue-west-town-downtown/).

Street safety advocates are skeptical. Sam Kovnar of the Northwest Neighborhood Alliance characterized the bus lane proposal as "a distraction from not wanting construction to happen at all."

The coalition's stated concerns include parking loss and winter maintenance problems — neither trivial, but both familiar objections from Phase 1, which was completed between Chicago and Damen avenues and now includes concrete-protected lanes, bus boarding islands, raised crosswalks, widened sidewalks, and new trees.

Phase 2 is still slated for 2026. CDOT hasn't indicated it plans to reverse course.

## Portage Park: Strong Turnout, Results Still Pending

The [Portage Park Neighborhood Bike Network](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/sites/complete-streets-chicago/home/featured-projects-and-innovations/projects0/PortageParkNeighborhoodBikeNetwork.html) survey has closed. CDOT collected more than **500 responses** before the deadline, and over 200 people showed up to the March 5 community meeting at the Portage Park Senior Center — the line stretched into the parking lot.

The proposed network includes paint-only bike lanes on Central, Laramie, and Montrose avenues, plus neighborhood greenways on Sunnyside, Berteau, Melvina, Menard, and Long avenues. CDOT's Brad Huff described opinions as "pretty mixed," though the crowd at the March 5 meeting skewed supportive. Notably, [Streetsblog Chicago's coverage](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2026/03/06/bikeway-hearing-parking-lot-talking-with-folks-from-the-massive-crowd-outside-and-inside-the-portage-park-neighborhood-bike-network-meeting) found a common thread among supporters: safety, not recreation. "It feels like this is something that I want to support," said one Long Avenue resident, citing a teen cyclist fatality on his block.

No survey results have been published. CDOT will hold additional community meetings before any installation decision. Target window: **late 2026 or early 2027**.

# Lead

## Federal Loans, Still Largely Parked

A September 2025 [WBEZ investigation](https://www.wbez.org/environment/2025/09/22/lead-pipes-chicago-city-council-notification-federal-loans-unspent) found that millions in federal loans to replace Chicago's lead service lines remain largely unspent. The federal math is unforgiving: Chicago has more than **412,000** confirmed and suspected lead service lines — the most of any U.S. city — and at current replacement pace, the city won't finish until around **2076**, roughly 30 years past the EPA's [Lead and Copper Rule Improvements](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/final-lead-and-copper-rule-improvements) deadline.

As noted in [last week's post](/primary-eve-bike-lobby-rat-council/), the Department of Water Management is also seeking a **$28 million Illinois EPA loan** separate from the federal WIFIA funds. Even if approved, NRDC has calculated that Chicago needs to accelerate replacements from ~8,300 to ~20,000 per year to meet the federal timeline — a tripling of pace that no current funding commitment appears to achieve.

# Vegan

## Another Chapter in Chicago's Vegan Restaurant Winter

The closures keep coming. **Bloom Plant Based Kitchen** (Wicker Park) shuttered on February 21, 2026 — its final service was Valentine's Day. TimeOut had named it one of the city's best vegan restaurants. The team has hinted a new vegan venture may be coming, so this may be more of a metamorphosis than a death.

**Kitchen 17** (Avondale), home of the original vegan deep-dish pizza, closed in November 2025 after 13 years. CBS Chicago reported that [Chicago lost four vegan or vegetarian restaurants in just eight days](https://www.cbsnews.com/chicago/news/chicago-loses-four-vegan-restaurants-in-just-eight-days/) in a recent stretch.

The [Chicago Tribune analyzed the wave in January](https://www.chicagobusiness.com/restaurants/chicago-plant-based-restaurants-close-diners-return-meat): the reasons are complicated. Post-pandemic consumer behavior, inflation, and the cultural cycling of food trends all play a role. Experts caution against reading the moment as a verdict on plant-based food broadly — but for a city that briefly felt like a vegan dining destination, the losses sting.

# Housing & Abundance

## BUILD Introduced; Primary Results Will Shape Its Fate

[HB 5626](https://ilga.gov/Legislation/BillStatus?DocNum=5626&GAID=18&DocTypeID=HB&SessionID=114), Rep. Kam Buckner's enabling legislation for Gov. Pritzker's [BUILD plan](https://capitolnewsillinois.com/news/pritzker-to-propose-statewide-zoning-laws-to-spur-homebuilding-limit-local-control/), has been formally introduced and is working through the spring session. [Municipal Minute published a detailed introduction](https://municipalminute.ancelglink.com/2026/03/introduction-of-build-plan-legislation.html) of the legislation this month. No floor vote is expected before April.

The Metropolitan Mayors Caucus continues to advocate for a housing task force before any preemption vote — municipalities don't love being told what to zone. Today's primary results, particularly in the state House and Senate, will help determine whether the votes are there.

## ADU Countdown: 15 Days

The citywide ADU ordinance takes effect **April 1**. RT and RM zones can begin submitting permit applications that day. [Chicago Cityscape](https://www.chicagocityscape.com/adu/) has the tracker; the [city's ADU page](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/sites/additional-dwelling-units-ordinance/home.html) has the details. Single-family (RS) zones still require aldermanic opt-in.
