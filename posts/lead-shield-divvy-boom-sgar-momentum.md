# Lead

## Federal Judge Shields Illinois Lead Funding

On March 13, a federal judge issued a preliminary injunction blocking the Trump administration's attempt to cut more than **$600 million in CDC public health grants** to Illinois and three other states. The proposed cuts would have eliminated lead poisoning prevention grants to 25 local health departments and wiped out roughly 100 IDPH positions statewide. The injunction is holding, but the underlying legal fight — Illinois Attorney General Kwame Raoul sued the federal government in February — is ongoing.

This is meaningful for Chicago specifically. The city's [Childhood Lead Poisoning Prevention Program](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/cdph/provdrs/environ_health/svcs/childhood_lead_poisoning_preventionprogram.html) depends on a pipeline of state and federal funding that flows partly through the grants now under threat. The service line replacement program similarly relies on federal infrastructure money that, while separate from CDC grants, operates in the same politically volatile funding environment.

The injunction is a reprieve, not a resolution. # Bikes

## Divvy Is Getting Bigger — and Pricier

There's genuinely good news on the Divvy front: on March 16, the city [announced 200+ new or upgraded stations in 2026](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/mayor/press_room/press_releases/2026/march/divvy-bike-expansion.html), with roughly 100 entirely new locations and 100 upgrades to existing docks. The expansion explicitly targets equity gaps on the **South, West, and Northwest sides** — neighborhoods that have historically had thin coverage relative to downtown and the North Side. A $99 annual membership is on offer for new and lapsed riders; current members get a price freeze.

The catch: per-minute and day-pass prices went up effective March 11, making casual use more expensive. For everyday riders locked into annual passes, the expansion is a straightforward win. For occasional users, the math is getting tighter. [Block Club Chicago (March 17)](https://blockclubchicago.org/2026/03/17/divvy-to-add-over-200-stations-across-chicago-from-downtown-to-far-south-side/) · [Chicago Sun-Times (March 16)](https://chicago.suntimes.com/transportation/2026/03/16/divvy-bicycle) · [WGN TV on price increases](https://wgntv.com/news/chicago-news/divvy-announces-price-increases-in-chicago/)

## The Archer Political Sideshow

An interesting wrinkle has emerged in the ongoing Archer Avenue bike lane dispute: [South Side Weekly reports](https://southsideweekly.com/archer-ave-bike-lanes-tension-fuels-political-ambitions/) that the Archer Guardians anti-lane coalition has become something of a political launching pad, with aspiring candidates using the community protests as campaign infrastructure ahead of 2027 aldermanic races. The lanes themselves remain installed; CDOT has not announced design changes. Whether the political energy around Archer translates into electoral consequences — or simply dissipates once the new traffic patterns become the new normal — is worth watching.

Meanwhile, Grand Avenue Phase 2 opposition (Damen to Ogden, West Town) is still organized and pushing for a shared bus-bike configuration instead of protected lanes. No CDOT response yet.

# Wildlife

## SGARs: The Momentum Is Building

Last week we noted Australia's ban on consumer-sale SGARs (second-generation anticoagulant rodenticides — the "supermarket rat poison" that kills owls, hawks, and coyotes up the food chain). This week, add **Colorado** to the list: [SB062](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/sb25-062), which would designate SGARs as restricted-use pesticides in that state, passed its Senate Third Reading on March 18. Colorado joins Connecticut (restricted-use as of January 2026) and Rhode Island (ban bill pending) in the regional trend.

Illinois still has no SGAR bill filed in Springfield. Chicago sits at a major raptor migration corridor — and the session runs through May. The window remains open.

The rat oral contraceptive pilot in Lincoln Park (launched August 2025, in partnership with Lincoln Park Zoo and the Chicago Bird Alliance) continues, with initial data expected this summer. # Housing & Abundance

## ADU Permit Day: 11 Days Out

A quick countdown update: the permanent ADU ordinance takes effect **April 1** — permit applications open the same day. Notably, the city has [paused new permit applications since March 18](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/sites/additional-dwelling-units-ordinance/home.html) while the system transitions to the new rules. The [Chicago Cityscape ADU tracker](https://www.chicagocityscape.com/adu/) will be the best place to watch how much pent-up demand actually materializes once the window opens.

HB 5626 (the statewide BUILD Plan) remains in the House Rules Committee with no floor vote scheduled.

Mayor Johnson's formal veto of the tip credit freeze has still not been transmitted as of March 21, though he pledged it publicly last Wednesday. The Council voted 30-18 — four short of the 34 needed to override. Nothing to do but watch.
