# City Council: The Morning After

## Tip Credit Freeze: 30-18, But Johnson Will Veto

Yesterday's main event delivered exactly the outcome many observers expected: the City Council voted **30-18** to freeze Chicago's subminimum wage for tipped workers at its current level — $12.62/hour, or 76% of the full $16.60 minimum wage — halting the One Fair Wage phase-down schedule that would have reached full parity by 2028.

The number matters: 30 is **four short** of the 34 needed to override a veto. Mayor Johnson called the vote "shameful" at a post-Council press conference and confirmed he will issue his third mayoral veto. With the override threshold out of reach, the veto is expected to be sustained — meaning the One Fair Wage timeline likely resumes, and tipped workers' wages continue climbing toward parity.

The ordinance was discharged to the floor via a Rule 41 motion led by Ald. Samantha Nugent (39th) and Ald. Bennett Lawson (44th); it had never moved through committee in the ordinary way. The restaurant industry and hospitality workers' unions offered predictably competing testimony about whether a freeze protects jobs or exploits workers. The answer, as usual, depends substantially on who you ask — and what evidence you weight.

## COPA Gets Welcoming City Teeth

The other major vote was unambiguous: the Council passed the COPA amendment empowering the [Civilian Office of Police Accountability](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/copa.html) to investigate CPD officers for alleged violations of the Welcoming City Ordinance. COPA had already received **44 complaints** related to CPD conduct during federal immigration enforcement activity since June 2025, but its jurisdictional authority to act on them had been legally murky. That ambiguity is now resolved. Mayor Johnson and Police Superintendent Larry Snelling both supported the measure, which was championed by Ald. Jessie Fuentes (26th) and Ald. Andre Vasquez (40th).

The same Council session confirmed **LaKenya White** as permanent Chief Administrator of COPA — a position she had been filling in an interim capacity — with a unanimous vote.

## Other Council Business

- A **Medicare for All resolution** passed, introduced by Ald. Ruth Cruz (30th), calling on Congress to pass H.R. 3069 / S. 1506. Resolutions are non-binding, but they're a useful gauge of Council sentiment — and an on-record statement ahead of the general election.
- The Council established a **Proactive Rental Inspection Working Group**, led by Ald. Rodriguez-Sanchez.
- A **Tourism Improvement District** ordinance passed.
- The **$27 million settlement** for the family of Stacy Vaughn-Harrell — killed when a driver being chased by CPD crashed into her vehicle in 2017 — was also approved.

# Bikes

## The Capital Cliff Nobody's Talking About

The Archer Ave backlash and Grand Ave business coalition drama get more ink, but here's the structural story underneath: Chicago's capital program slashes Complete Streets and Vision Zero funding by roughly **70%**, from $791 million to $212 million, largely to cover firefighter back-pay and police misconduct settlements. The practical result: [Streetsblog Chicago reports](https://chi.streetsblog.org/2025/12/10/documents-show-the-main-funding-source-for-crash-prevention-dropping-from-791m-to-212m-to-help-cover-firefighter-back-pay-and-police-misconduct-settlements) the city is projecting only **23 miles of bikeway projects in 2026**, including just 7 miles of protected lanes — down from 50+ miles in 2023 and 2024.

Grand Ave Phase 2 (Damen to Ogden) and the Portage Park Neighborhood Bike Network are both technically on the 2026 list. Whether the funding is actually there for both is a question worth watching.

The Portage Park survey has closed (500+ responses); CDOT will announce next steps before any installation begins, with a late-2026 or early-2027 target window.

# Lead

## The Clock Ticks, The Loan Sits

No new headlines in the past 24 hours, but the structural problem is worth restating: Chicago has replaced [roughly 8,000-14,000](https://insideclimatenews.org/news/26062025/chicago-lead-pipe-replacement-plan/) of its **412,000 lead service lines** since 2021 — the most of any city in the country. To meet the EPA's Lead and Copper Rule Improvements deadline, the city needs to hit ~20,000 replacements/year starting in 2027. The current pace won't get there. Meanwhile, most of Chicago's **$325 million WIFIA loan** remains undrawn. Congress has already clipped $125 million from the federal lead pipe replacement program (trimmed from a proposed $250M cut after Democratic pushback), and the Trump EPA's budget would gut the low-interest loan infrastructure that states depend on. Advocates at [NRDC](https://www.nrdc.org/bio/chakena-d-sims/federal-clock-ticking-actions-chicago-must-take-to-get-lead-out) are urging Chicago to "front-end load" replacements before the bipartisan infrastructure law funds expire.

# Housing & Abundance

## ADU Ordinance: 13 Days

The citywide ADU ordinance takes effect **April 1** — 13 days from today. The full picture: ADUs are allowed as-of-right in all RT and RM multifamily districts citywide (excluding the downtown core); aldermanic opt-in is still required for single-family RS zones outside the original pilot areas; and a union apprenticeship labor requirement narrows the contractor pool for coach house construction.

[Chicago Cityscape's ADU tracker](https://www.chicagocityscape.com/adu/) has permit application details as they become available.

**BUILD plan (HB 5626)** remains in the House Rules Committee. Yesterday's primary results — Stratton's Senate win, Daniel Biss heading to Congress from the 9th, and a new House/Senate composition to be certified — will shape the bill's prospects when it comes up for a floor vote, no earlier than April.
