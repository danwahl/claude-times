# Wildlife

## Colorado SB26-062: Dead on Arrival in the House

The news is bad for rodenticide reform in Colorado. **SB26-062** — the bill that would have restricted second-generation anticoagulant rodenticides (SGARs), first-generation anticoagulants, and glue traps — appears to have been killed today, March 30, in the House. Bill tracking services show it reached the "House Second Reading Calendar" with status **Dead**, the standard Colorado mechanism for a floor kill via "postponed indefinitely." The bill had cleared the full Senate 22–12 on March 18 after a hard-fought committee battle, only to succumb to fierce agricultural opposition in a House chamber that has historically been less hospitable to animal welfare legislation.

It is worth pausing on what this loss means. Colorado was one of the few live legislative prospects for SGAR reform in 2026 — the federal EPA has been slow-walking its amended decision on rodenticide registrations, California's Poison-Free Wildlife Act (AB 2552) is the national high-water mark, and most states are watching rather than acting. Colorado's Senate passage was a genuine achievement; the House did what the agricultural industry lobbied it to do.

For Chicagoans: the city's wildlife advocates at the Chicago Bird Alliance are still pushing for a cut to the city's ~$14.6M rodenticide budget and funding for contraceptive rat control. A peer-reviewed study in *Science of the Total Environment* found that **100% of sampled Chicago urban mesopredators** — raccoons, skunks, opossums — tested positive for at least one anticoagulant rodenticide compound, a damning baseline that isn't going anywhere regardless of what happens in Denver.

# Bikes & Transit

## Violet Harris: Nine Days, No Arrest

It has now been **nine days** since 15-year-old **Violet Harris** was killed by a wrong-way hit-and-run driver at 77th and Exchange in South Shore, while riding a Lime e-scooter. CPD's Major Accident Detectives continue to investigate; the crash was captured on a Police Observation Device camera. No arrest, no public suspect description.

Harris is the sixth vulnerable road user killed on Chicago streets in 2026. Streetsblog Chicago's pointed critique of the Tribune's framing — which centered e-scooter risk over driver accountability — remains the most cogent account of what this case reveals about how Chicago assigns responsibility for street violence.

If you have information about this crash, CPD's tip line is 312-745-TIPS.

## HB 2454: The Bike Liability Fight in Springfield

A less-reported but consequential fight is unfolding at the state level. **HB 2454** would overturn a 1998 Illinois Supreme Court precedent that classified cyclists as merely "permitted" road users — a legal distinction that has long shielded municipalities from liability for injuries sustained in bike crashes. Ride Illinois and Bike Grid Now! are pushing the bill hard. The City of Chicago, somewhat remarkably, is lobbying *against* it, citing potential liability costs.

Advocates have crunched the numbers and find the city's argument weak: Chicago paid roughly $666K in road injury claims in the first nine months of 2025; the proportional cost for bicycle trips would be around $22K annually. Reclassifying cyclists as "intended" road users would give injured cyclists better legal recourse and create a modest financial incentive for the city to build protected infrastructure — which is arguably the entire point.

No vote date has been announced. Worth watching.

# Local

## ADU Portal Opens Tomorrow

A reminder that's also an invitation: Chicago's permanent ADU ordinance takes effect **Tuesday, April 1**, and the application portal opens that morning. If you're in an RT or RM zoning district (most multifamily neighborhoods), you can now apply for a coach house or interior conversion unit citywide. Single-family RS zones remain aldermanic opt-in, with 28 wards currently enrolled.

Key things to know before you apply:
- Average processing time is ~121 days from application submission
- Coach house contractors must use union apprentice labor
- ADUs cannot be used as short-term rentals
- Properties with 2+ ADUs: 50% must be affordable at 60% AMI

[ADU application portal](https://webapps4.chicago.gov/eforms/aduapplication) · [Chicago Cityscape explainer](https://www.chicagocityscape.com/adu/)

## Tipped Wage Veto: Override Looks Unlikely April 15

On March 25, Mayor Brandon Johnson vetoed the City Council's 30-18 vote to freeze the phase-out of the tipped minimum wage — his third veto in office. The override vote is scheduled for **April 15** and requires 34 votes, four more than the council mustered the first time. Early whip counts from the Sun-Times suggest swing votes (Ald. La Spata, Robinson, Vasquez, Martin) are not moving; the veto appears likely to hold, keeping Chicago's One Fair Wage Ordinance — which phases out the tipped minimum wage by July 1, 2028, with 8% annual raises in the interim — on track.

The political geography here is interesting. Johnson's veto came as Chicago's restaurant industry is citing 496 restaurant closures in the first half of 2025. The mayor frames the tipped wage as a vestige of exploitation; restaurateurs frame it as the straw that breaks the camel's back. Both things can be true. Readers who want to weigh in before April 15 can contact their alderperson.

# Lead

## Lead Pipe Replacement Could Support 90,000 Illinois Jobs

A March 23 analysis by MKM Consultants — commissioned with Current, Elevate, and HIRE360 — finds that replacing Illinois's approximately **1.5 million known or suspected lead service lines** could support up to **90,000 jobs** over a decade. The catch: only if the state closes a $5–9B funding gap beyond the ~$1B available from the federal infrastructure law.

The Chicago-specific numbers are sobering. The city has **412,000 known lead lines** and has replaced roughly 8,000 since 2021 — a pace that implies a **50+ year timeline** at current rates, which is to say, never. The city averaged $30,000 per replacement and has drawn only $70–90M of a $325M federal loan that expires next year. A February NRDC report called out Chicago for missing a federal Lead and Copper Rule notification requirement and urged an acceleration to ~20,000 replacements per year.

The jobs analysis adds a dimension that tends to cut across political lines: lead pipe replacement is an infrastructure investment with measurable local employment returns. Whether that framing opens any new advocacy doors in Springfield or at City Hall remains to be seen.

## Still Waiting

Congress is on Easter recess; members return around April 7. The House floor vote on **H.R. 7567** remains unscheduled. The stalemate — MAHA-aligned Republicans opposed to pesticide liability shield provisions, Democrats opposed to SNAP impacts — hasn't shifted. The 2018 Farm Bill extension expires **September 30, 2026**, which is the only real deadline in sight. GovTrack puts passage odds at 11%.
