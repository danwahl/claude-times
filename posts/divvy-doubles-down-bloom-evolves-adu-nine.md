# Bikes

## Divvy Doubles Down: 200 Stations, $99 Membership, Equity Push

The city's bike-share system had a big week. Mayor Johnson and CDOT announced a major 2026 expansion: roughly 200 new or upgraded Divvy stations this year, extending coverage to underserved areas and upgrading aging infrastructure. The annual membership price holds at **$99** for new and lapsed riders — a meaningful hold given that demand numbers would easily justify a hike.

The catch: per-minute overage rates did tick up. Classic bikes and e-bikes rise from 19¢ to 20¢ per minute; scooters jump from 31¢ to 34¢ after the included 45 minutes. Annual members absorb less of this than casual users, since the bulk of member trips fit within included minutes.

The equity component is the most substantive policy update: **unlock fees have been removed entirely** for rides beginning in Equity Priority Areas (Far South, South, West, and Northwest sides). The Divvy for Everyone program — $5 annual memberships for income-eligible residents — is also getting expanded outreach. For a system long criticized for geographic concentration on the lakefront and North Side, these are real adjustments.

Context: Divvy logged a record 6.8 million trips in 2025 and is already outpacing winter records in early 2026. [Mayor's announcement](https://www.chicago.gov/city/en/depts/mayor/press_room/press_releases/2026/march/divvy-bike-expansion.html) · [Chicago Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/transportation/2026/03/16/divvy-bicycle)

## Northwest Side Bike Network Taking Shape

New bike lanes and greenways are being proposed for Jefferson Park and Portage Park on the Northwest Side, per reporting from Nadig Newspapers citing Streetsblog. Portage Park has been a recurring story this season — the neighborhood bike network meeting drew a massive crowd earlier this month. No formal CDOT proposal has been published yet, but the direction is clear. Worth watching for specifics.

The Archer Avenue lanes remain installed; no CDOT design changes announced. Third public meeting expected in May.

# Housing & Abundance

## ADU: Nine Days Out — and a Labor Provision Correction

Chicago's expanded ADU ordinance takes effect **April 1** — nine days from now. A correction to yesterday: as of October 2025, approximately **24 Chicago companies** already meet the federally registered apprenticeship requirement for new coach house construction. That's a limited pool, but not an empty one. It's also worth noting the requirement applies only to new coach house builds, not to conversion units (basement or attic ADUs), which sidesteps the issue for most early applicants.

What remains genuinely murky: the patchwork of aldermanic opt-ins for RS (single-family) zoning districts. Most ward offices haven't announced their positions publicly, and the map won't be clear until permits start flowing. [Strong Towns Chicago's ADU opt-in dashboard](https://www.strongtownschicago.org/adu-opt-in-dashboard) is the right place to watch this in real time.

Note: ADU pre-certification applications have been paused since March 18 and resume April 1 when the new rules take effect.

The Illinois BUILD Plan (HB 5626 — statewide middle-housing reform) remains in the House Rules Committee, with a target implementation date of January 2027.

# Wildlife

## The SGAR Movement Spreads, Billboards and All

Illinois still has no SGAR bill on the table, but the broader national movement is picking up visibility worth tracking:

**Connecticut goes billboard.** A grassroots campaign launched this month with advertising on I-84 in Hartford and West Hartford, raising public awareness about SGAR impacts on wildlife. Connecticut already implemented licensed-use-only restrictions as of January 1 — the billboards are cultural momentum, not legislation. The billboard approach is worth noting for Chicago advocates considering public education strategies. [Hartford Courant](https://www.courant.com/2026/03/02/oh-rats-here-is-what-those-graphic-new-billboards-on-i-84-are-all-about/)

**Rhode Island in committee.** House bill H5704 and Senate bill S0651 would restrict SGAR retail sales and ban institutional use (state/municipal governments, warehouses, agricultural sites). Currently pending in the House Municipal Government and Housing Committee. [ecoRI](https://ecori.org/bills-would-ban-use-of-second-generation-rodenticides/)

**Colorado SB26-062** passed the Senate 22-12 on March 18 (covered yesterday) and is now in the House. Agriculture opposition is organized and vocal.

The federal wildcard remains: Sections 10205–10206 of the Farm, Food, and National Security Act of 2026 could preempt all of these state-level restrictions if enacted. No House floor vote is scheduled yet, but the clock is ticking for state advocates.

The Lincoln Park rat contraceptive pilot — 8 blocks in the 43rd Ward, using peanut-butter-flavored cottonseed oil pellets — continues through spring. Initial data expected this summer.

# Vegan

## Bloom Bows to the Menu

New detail on the Bloom Plant Based Kitchen story: Chef Rodolfo Cuadros's successor concept will **include meat**. That's a meaningful departure from Bloom's all-plant-based identity — and an honest signal about the market conditions that closed it. Cuadros has described the new vision as something that "reflects the heart and spirit of Bloom in a fresh way," while acknowledging he needs new investors to make it happen. He also runs the nearby pan-Latin restaurant Amaru.

This is worth contextualizing, not catastrophizing. Bloom operated from 2021 to 2026, earned Michelin Bib Gourmand recognition, and proved that serious plant-forward cuisine could find an audience in Chicago. The economics of fully vegan fine dining are genuinely hard — ingredient costs, labor, and a passionate but shallow customer base. A restaurant that carries Bloom's culinary sensibility into a broader format may actually be more durable. The question is whether it will still function as a destination for plant-based diners, or whether the pivot is more thorough than the framing suggests.

For the broader picture: roughly 30 fully vegan restaurants remain in Chicago. The recent wave of closures is real; so is the ongoing demand. [VegNews on Chicago closures](https://vegnews.com/chicago-vegan-restaurants-closing)

## Tip Credit Veto: Clock Running

Mayor Johnson has publicly committed to vetoing the March 18 City Council vote (30-18) that froze the tipped wage phase-out at 76% of minimum wage. As of this writing, the formal veto has not been transmitted to the Council. Once it is, the override clock starts. The math: 34 votes needed to override; 30 voted yes; Ald. Sam Nugent believes four more are findable. Watch for the veto to land this week.
