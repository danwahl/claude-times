# Vegan & Restaurant News

## Bloom's Replacement Won't Be Vegan

When Bloom Plant Based Kitchen shut its doors at 1559 N. Milwaukee on February 21, Chef Rodolfo Cuadros signaled that a new concept would follow. That concept now has a name and a date: **Port Union** is scheduled for a **soft opening April 17** in the same Wicker Park space. The menu still foregrounds vegetarian options — but adds meat dishes, marking a deliberate departure from the fully plant-based model Cuadros built at Bloom.

It's a familiar arc for Chicago's vegan scene. Bloom joins Kitchen 17, Native Foods, Chicago Raw, and Chicago Diner's Logan Square location in a wave of closures since late 2025. The Chicago Diner's original Halsted/Lakeview location remains open; Veggie House in Chinatown is still operating. By HappyCow's count, roughly 30 fully vegan restaurants remain in the city.

The Port Union pivot is at least good news for the real estate: a restaurant in the space beats a vacancy, and vegetarian-leaning menus serve the neighborhood even if they're no longer a vegan destination. But the shrinking count of all-vegan options in a city this size is worth watching.

# Bikes & Road Safety

## Violet Harris: Two Weeks, Still No Arrest

Fourteen days have passed since **Violet Harris**, 15, was struck and killed by a wrong-way hit-and-run driver at 77th and Exchange in South Shore while riding a Lime e-scooter on March 21. CPD's Major Accident Detectives have made no arrest, despite the incident being captured on a Police Observation Device camera and a 13-year-old companion surviving to provide an account.

New Block Club reporting adds sobering context: Harris's death was the **83rd hit-and-run on South Shore's main corridors in 2026** — one of 248 total crashes since January 1. One in three drivers flees after a crash on these streets.

That ratio isn't a crime-wave statistic so much as an infrastructure one. Hit-and-run rates fall when drivers feel accountable, and accountability requires both enforcement and road design that slows traffic and narrows the window for impunity. Neither is in obvious supply on South Shore's major arterials.

On a smaller note: CDOT launched a new **flooded bikeway reporting tool** on April 3, allowing riders to flag waterlogged bike infrastructure for faster city response during spring. It's a useful operational tool — and a reminder that CDOT can move quickly when the fix is a software form rather than concrete.

# Wildlife

## Colorado SB26-062: Heard but Not Yet Decided

Colorado's **SB26-062** — the bill restricting second-generation anticoagulant rodenticides (SGARs), first-generation anticoagulants, and glue traps — cleared the first House gauntlet intact. On March 30, the House Agriculture, Water & Natural Resources Committee held a full testimony session but did not call a vote; the bill remains alive and pending. The committee's next scheduled meetings are **April 6 and April 13**.

The bill passed the full Senate 22–12 on March 18. A House committee vote was the immediate obstacle; if it clears, it faces a chamber floor that has historically been less receptive to animal welfare legislation than the Senate. Agricultural opposition has been organized and vocal throughout.

For Illinois readers, the outcome has indirect relevance: a Colorado win would add to the growing state-level body of law restricting SGARs, strengthening the case for similar action in Springfield and Chicago. It would also be a loss for the pest control industry's argument that no alternative models exist.

Colorado residents can still submit written testimony at the [bill's official page](https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/SB26-062).

# Abundance

## Jackson Park Pilot and Chicago Rebuild Launch Tomorrow

Two modest but meaningful programs kick off **Sunday, April 6**:

- **Jackson Park Tenant Opportunity to Purchase pilot**: Tenants in buildings with 10+ units within the Jackson Park Pilot Area (roughly 60th–71st, Martin Luther King Dr. to Lake Shore Dr.) gain the right of first refusal when their building lists for sale. The program is a test of community ownership tools ahead of potential citywide expansion.
- **Chicago Rebuild Pilot**: $3 million in support for rehab of vacant 1–4 unit buildings within the same area, with finished units priced for buyers at up to 120% AMI.

Neither program is citywide, but both expand the toolkit for residents trying to stay in place as development pressure builds around Jackson Park.

The ADU portal continues to accept applications — no updated count since the 30+ reported in the first 12 hours of April 1. The next major housing engagement is a **ChiBlockBuilder webinar Tuesday, April 7 at 1 p.m.** for buyers interested in the 600+ city-owned market-rate lots that opened this week.
