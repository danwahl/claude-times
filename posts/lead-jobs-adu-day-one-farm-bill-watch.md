# Lead Service Lines

## The Jobs Angle

A new report, covered by WBEZ, Inside Climate News, and Grist, reframes Chicago's 400,000-pipe lead service line backlog as something other than just a fiscal and public health catastrophe: it could be a major workforce engine.

The analysis, released March 23 by public health and workforce development advocates, estimates that fully replacing Illinois's lead service lines over a decade could support up to **90,000 jobs** — plumbers, pipefitters, excavation crews, inspectors, administrative staff. The catch is familiar: the job creation only materializes if replacement actually accelerates, which so far it hasn't.

Chicago's official timeline remains 2076 — a half-century away — compared to the EPA's accelerated 10-year target. The city has spent only a fraction of its $325M federal infrastructure loan, and as of mid-2025 had sent required notifications to fewer than 10% of the approximately 900,000 affected households. That pace is hard to square with the urgency the public health record demands.

The report's political utility is real: framing lead remediation as a jobs bill rather than only a regulatory burden could attract different coalitions in Springfield and Congress. Whether that reframing translates into accelerated funding and permitting remains to be seen.

# Housing & Abundance

## ADU Portal: Day One

Yesterday's ADU expansion — the headline of our April 1 issue — appears to have gotten off to a live start. The application portal at the Chicago Department of Housing began accepting submissions citywide as of April 1, with properties in multifamily zoning districts (RT/RM) outside the downtown core now eligible to apply as-of-right. More than 600 city-owned lots also hit ChiBlockBuilder simultaneously.

A few near-term dates to flag for readers who want to engage:

- **April 6**: Jackson Park Pilot Area tenant right of first purchase begins
- **April 7, 1 p.m.**: ChiBlockBuilder informational webinar for market-rate buyers
- **April 13**: Missing Middle housing webinar (West Englewood and South Chicago lots, with up to $150,000/unit in construction assistance and $1 parcel prices)

Reminder: DOH pre-certification (now accepting applications) is a prerequisite for ADU permits but a separate process from the Department of Buildings permit. Processing times have been running about 120 days.

# Farm Bill

## Congress Returns Tuesday

The House floor vote on **H.R. 7567** slipped past its pre-Easter target. Congress returns from recess around April 7, and Chairman GT Thompson has said to expect a floor vote that week — roughly April 7–11. The political math hasn't improved: MAHA Republicans remain wary of the pesticide liability shield in Section 10205; most Democrats are opposed over SNAP cuts. Floor passage is uncertain, and Senate action is further off still.

For Chicago readers, the stakes include: SNAP enrollment for roughly 800,000 Illinois households, a 95% Buy American mandate for school lunch programs, and the fate of commodity and conservation programs that shape what's grown and priced nationally.
