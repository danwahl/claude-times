# Bikes & Transit

## HB 2454: Dead for This Session

The bill that would have reclassified Illinois cyclists as "intended" rather than merely "permitted" road users — improving their legal standing in injury cases — was **re-referred to the House Rules Committee on March 27**, the procedural equivalent of a quiet burial. This ends the push for this session.

The path had seemed plausible: the bill cleared the Judiciary-Civil Committee and the Civil Procedure & Tort Liability Subcommittee before getting kicked back to Rules. The City of Chicago's active lobbying against HB 2454 — citing liability costs that cycling advocates estimated at around $22,000 annually — now stands as the decisive factor in its failure.

To recap what's lost: a 1998 Illinois Supreme Court precedent classifies cyclists as permitted road users, limiting municipal liability for injuries sustained in crashes. Reclassification as intended users would give injured cyclists better legal recourse and give municipalities a financial incentive to build protected infrastructure. Ride Illinois and Bike Grid Now! have vowed to return next session.

## Violet Harris: Ten Days, No Arrest — One of 83

It has been **ten days** since 15-year-old Violet Harris was killed by a hit-and-run driver at 77th and Exchange in South Shore. No arrest, no public suspect description.

Block Club Chicago added important context Monday: Harris was killed in **one of 83 hit-and-run crashes on South Shore's main roads this year alone**. That figure reframes the tragedy from an isolated incident into what it actually is — one data point in an epidemic of unaccountable road violence. The pattern on these corridors is documented. The response has not matched the scale of the problem.

If you have information about this crash, CPD's tip line is **312-745-TIPS**.

# Wildlife

## City Council Introduces Bird-Friendly Building Design Ordinance

A notable piece of legislation arrived at the March 18 City Council meeting that deserves more attention than it's received: **O2026-0024083**, an amendment to Municipal Code Title 13 that would establish requirements for **bird-friendly building design**. The ordinance was introduced and referred to committee; no hearing date has been set.

Bird-window collisions kill an estimated **1 billion birds annually** in the United States. Chicago sits directly on the Mississippi Flyway and is consistently ranked among the most dangerous cities for migratory birds — the Field Museum, McCormick Place (now retrofitted), and a wall of reflective Loop towers have all claimed significant casualties. A mandatory bird-safe design standard would bring Chicago closer to cities like San Francisco and New York, which have adopted similar rules covering new construction and major renovations.

The Chicago Bird Alliance has been central to this push. Worth watching as it works through committee.

## Rat Contraceptive: Early Wins in Wicker Park

A March 24 Block Club Chicago report brings welcome news from the Wicker Park/Bucktown pilot of SenesTech's "Evolve" contraceptive bait: **early results are positive**. The Special Service Area No. 33 pilot — deployed along North Avenue, Ashland, Milwaukee, and Division — is showing reduced rat activity, per the SSA's assessment.

The companion Lincoln Park pilot, run in partnership with the Chicago Bird Alliance and Lincoln Park Zoo's Urban Wildlife Institute (43rd Ward, $40K budget, started August 2025), is expected to publish data in spring or summer 2026.

Both pilots are driven in part by a grim wildlife reality: Chicago's urban mesopredators test at 100% positive for anticoagulant rodenticide compounds, and raptor deaths from secondary poisoning — including an entire Great Horned Owl family in Lincoln Park — have made the human cost of conventional rodenticide visible to residents in a way that raw statistics often don't.

# Local

## ADU Portal Opens Tomorrow

The moment abundance-agenda advocates have been anticipating: Chicago's permanent ADU ordinance takes effect **tomorrow, April 1**, and the application portal opens that morning. Citywide eligibility now covers all multifamily RT and RM zoning districts (excluding downtown). Single-family RS zones remain aldermanic opt-in, with 28 wards enrolled.

If you're eligible and considering applying: average processing time is ~121 days from submission. Coach houses require union apprentice labor; no short-term rentals permitted; properties adding 2+ ADUs must make 50% affordable at 60% AMI.

## Tipped Wage: Springfield Adds a New Threat

The fight over Chicago's One Fair Wage Ordinance — currently on track to phase out the tipped minimum wage by July 1, 2028, after Mayor Johnson's veto of the Council freeze — just acquired a second front. A bill by **Rep. Curtis Tarver (D-Chicago)** that would **strip Chicago's authority to regulate tipped wages at all** passed the Illinois House Labor and Commerce Committee **22-4**. If enacted, it would slash Chicago's tipped wage floor back to the state rate of $9/hour.

To be clear about the political geometry: this is a Democratic state legislator from Chicago advancing a bill that would preempt Chicago's own progressive labor ordinance, at the behest of the restaurant industry. The veto override vote on April 15 now sits alongside this Springfield bill as a two-front battle for tipped workers. Neither front looks decided.

# Farm Bill

## House Floor Vote Expected After Easter

Congress returns from Easter recess around **April 7**. The House floor vote on **H.R. 7567** (the Farm, Food, and National Security Act of 2026) — which passed committee 34-17 — is expected sometime in that week. The bill includes an Office of Biotechnology Policy at USDA but draws opposition from MAHA-aligned Republicans over pesticide liability shield provisions, and from Democrats over SNAP cuts. Senate passage would require 60 votes. GovTrack's 11% passage odds have not moved.
