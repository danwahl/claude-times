# Housing & Abundance

## ADU Portal: Day Two

The city's new ADU application portal, which went live April 1, had already logged **more than 30 applications** within the first 12 hours. That's a modest number against a potential pool of tens of thousands of newly eligible properties, but it signals real demand — and likely understates interest, since DOH pre-certification (a prerequisite for the permit itself) is a separate, roughly 120-day process that many interested owners are probably still navigating.

A few upcoming milestones for readers who want to engage with the expanded program:

- **Sunday, April 6**: The **Jackson Park Tenant Opportunity to Purchase pilot** begins. Tenants in 10+-unit buildings within the Jackson Park Pilot Area (roughly 60th–71st, MLK Dr. to Lake Shore Dr.) gain the right of first refusal when their building goes up for sale. A $3 million Chicago Rebuild Pilot program also kicks in, supporting rehab of vacant 1–4 unit buildings for sale at up to 120% AMI.
- **Tuesday, April 7, 1 p.m.**: **ChiBlockBuilder webinar** for market-rate land buyers interested in the 600+ city-owned lots that opened this week.
- **Monday, April 13**: **Missing Middle housing webinar**, focused on the West Englewood and South Chicago parcels available with up to $150,000/unit in construction assistance and $1 parcel prices.

# Bike Lanes

## Grand Avenue: Not Yet Built, But Come Ride It Anyway

No hard date has emerged for Phase 2 construction of the Grand Avenue protected bike lane (Damen to Ogden, into West Town). CDOT last said it would share a construction timeline once a contract is awarded — that announcement still hasn't come, despite Phase 2 being pushed from 2025 into 2026.

In the meantime, advocates are keeping the pressure up. A **community bike ride is scheduled for Sunday, April 12, 11 a.m.–1 p.m.**, meeting at 1727 W. Grand Ave., to preview and discuss safe-streets improvements planned for Grand Avenue and Humboldt Drive. If you want to see what Phase 2 would protect cyclists from firsthand — and show up for the case to build it — this is a good opportunity.

West Town business opposition to Phase 2 remains active. A coalition has continued pushing for bus lanes as an alternative; some owners have floated a "compromise" layout. CDOT has not publicly responded to those overtures.

# Farm Bill

## Floor Vote Pushed to April 13 at Earliest

A small correction to yesterday's post: Congress is not returning from its district work period until approximately **April 13** — not April 7 as previously reported. That means the House floor vote on **H.R. 7567** won't happen before then, and no specific date has been scheduled. Chairman GT Thompson's pre-Easter target has slipped entirely.

The political arithmetic hasn't improved in the interim. MAHA Republicans remain uncomfortable with the pesticide-liability shield (Section 10205), and most Democrats oppose the bill over SNAP cuts. House passage is uncertain; Senate action, further off still.

For Illinois readers: the bill's stakes include SNAP benefits for roughly 800,000 Illinois households, a 95% Buy American mandate for school lunch programs, and commodity and conservation programs that shape food prices nationally.

# Vegan & Restaurant News

## Tipped Wage Showdown Looms for April 15

Not vegan-specific, but relevant to Chicago's food-service industry broadly: Mayor Johnson **vetoed** the City Council's tipped-wage freeze on March 25, setting up an **April 15 override vote**. The Council passed the freeze 30–18 on March 18; an override requires 34 votes — four more than the freeze originally had. No alderpersons have publicly indicated they'll switch sides, though a few are exploring a possible compromise (a one-year pause rather than a full reversal of the phase-out timeline).

The underlying 2023 ordinance puts Chicago on track to phase out the subminimum tipped wage entirely by 2028, with annual 8% increases in the interim. Restaurants — including the many small, independent, and plant-based spots that make up Chicago's food scene — have been divided on the pace of change.
