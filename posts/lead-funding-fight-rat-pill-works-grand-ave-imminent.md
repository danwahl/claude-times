# Lead

## Illinois Pushes Back on Trump's Lead Pipe Money Freeze

Chicago needs to replace roughly 412,000 lead service lines — more than any other city in the country — and it has a loan to help do it. What it doesn't have is the federal grant money Congress already appropriated.

Illinois Rep. Raja Krishnamoorthi and other congressional Democrats sent a formal letter to the EPA this week alleging that approximately **$3 billion in FY2025 funds for lead pipe replacement have not been disbursed** to communities. "I think that they're playing games," Krishnamoorthi said, suggesting the delay is targeted at Democratic-led cities and states. Chicago received just $14 million for FY2025 and $28 million approved for FY2026 — sums that are, to be direct, rounding errors against the $12 billion the Chicago region alone needs for full remediation.

The city is still drawing from a $336 million Biden-era EPA WIFIA loan, with accelerated drawdowns planned for 2026 (targeting 10,000 replacements at ~$300 million). But the WIFIA loan is debt; the appropriated grants are what make the program affordable for low-income homeowners. Without them, the city can replace pipes in wealthier neighborhoods more easily than in the ones that need it most.

Compounding the picture: the Senate's current budget reconciliation package includes cuts of at least $125 million in lead pipe replacement funding nationally (the original House draft proposed $250 million). Congress appropriated the money. The administration isn't releasing it. Lawmakers say it's targeted. The EPA has not explained the delay.

# Wildlife

## Wicker Park's Rat Birth Control Is Working

Six months after the Lincoln Park rat contraceptive pilot launched, early results from a parallel Wicker Park/Bucktown program offer the first real Chicago validation that the approach works. As of late March, the Wicker Park chamber-run pilot is **showing measurable reductions in rat activity** — enough that organizers are already planning to expand within the neighborhood.

Both programs use bait boxes loaded with an extract of thunder god vine, an Asian plant whose active compound interrupts rodent reproduction (both sperm production and egg development) without the anticoagulant effects that have killed raptors — including a beloved family of great horned owls in Lincoln Park — throughout Chicago and beyond. Rats need to eat the bait every 30 to 60 days to remain infertile; the population gradually declines rather than spiking-and-crashing as with lethal rodenticides.

The Lincoln Park pilot, run by the Chicago Bird Alliance and Lincoln Park Zoo's Urban Wildlife Institute in the 43rd Ward, is still awaiting its first formal data readout (expected summer 2026), but Wicker Park's earlier and independently positive results lend credibility to the approach. The two pilots together are building exactly the real-world evidence base that advocates of Colorado SB26-062 — the SGAR restriction bill — cite when making the case that alternatives to second-generation anticoagulant rodenticides are viable in dense urban settings.

Speaking of which: SB26-062 had a House Agriculture, Water & Natural Resources Committee hearing in Denver today (1:30 p.m., Room 0107). No vote has been recorded as of this writing — the bill remains alive in committee, where it has been since passing the full Senate 22–12 on March 18. A prior March 30 hearing also concluded without a vote. We'll update when results are official.

# Bikes & Road Safety

## Grand Avenue Phase 2: Construction Is Coming Whether Businesses Want It or Not

The city has made its position official: the Phase 2 rebuild of Grand Avenue — a protected bike lane, bus boarding islands, and pedestrian bump-outs stretching from **Damen to Ogden** through West Town — is happening in 2026, and the design is not changing.

The announcement lands amid sustained opposition from a coalition of West Town and Ukrainian Village business owners, including Alliance Bakery's Peter Rios, who have argued the construction will wreck delivery truck access and accelerate a commercial corridor already under pressure. The Grand Avenue Committee has been organized around this argument for the better part of a year. CDOT's response, paraphrased: the redesign is locked. Construction is beginning. It's too late.

Supporters counter that Phase 1 of Grand Avenue (between Milwaukee and Damen) has performed well, and that the alternative — preserving door-zone parking at the expense of protected cycling infrastructure on a corridor that sees significant bike traffic — is its own kind of harm to businesses that depend on people arriving on foot or bike.

A **community bike ride** is scheduled for **April 12, 11 a.m. to 1 p.m.**, meeting at **1727 W. Grand**, to tour the Humboldt Drive and Grand Avenue improvements and discuss what's coming. It is a clear opportunity to show numbers in support of the project before construction starts.

Meanwhile, **no arrests** have been made in either of the two Hyde Park Academy hit-and-run fatalities covered in depth yesterday — Violet Harris (March 21, 77th and Exchange) or Lanai Smith (March 30, Dolton). The suspected vehicle in Smith's death — a 2007 red Chevrolet Corvette, Illinois plate **FR 56003** — has still not been located. CPD Major Accidents: 312-745-4521.

# Abundance

## ChiBlockBuilder Launches 600 Lots; ADU's First Week Posts 30+ Applications

The ADU ordinance went citywide April 1, and it didn't wait around: the DOH portal received **over 30 applications in the first 12 hours**. The expansion opens roughly **320,000 parcels** to ADU eligibility by right — coach houses, garden apartments, conversion units — up from about 116,000 under the pilot program. Aldermen retain significant local discretion over where ADUs land in their wards, giving the expansion a very Chicago flavor, but the default is now permissive rather than restrictive.

On the same day, **600+ city-owned vacant lots** hit the market via ChiBlockBuilder across 25 wards — the largest single inventory release since the program launched in 2022. The lots span a wide range: some pocket parcels priced at ~$5,000, a 2.6-acre Austin site large enough for up to 140 units, and 30 parcels in West Englewood and South Chicago explicitly reserved for Missing Middle housing (2–6 units), sold for **$1 each** with up to $150,000 in per-unit construction assistance. Total lot value: roughly $34 million.

**Also today, April 6:** The Jackson Park Tenant Opportunity to Purchase pilot officially takes effect, giving tenants in buildings between roughly 60th–71st streets and MLK Drive–Lake Shore Drive the right of first refusal when their landlord lists a property for sale.

# Vegan

## Veggie House Chinatown; A Sector Takes Stock

After a painful winter of closures — Kitchen 17, Native Foods Loop, Chicago Raw, the Chicago Diner Logan Square, and Bloom all shuttered between November 2025 and February 2026 — Chicago's plant-based dining scene has a few fresh reasons for optimism.

**Veggie House** in Chinatown is operating with April 2026 menus, bringing a new fully plant-based Chinese option to a neighborhood not historically known for vegan dining. In Evanston, **restaurant 17** (17 dishes, 17 seats, fully plant-based) opened at the Hilton Orrington in January and has been drawing attention as a high-concept counterweight to the casual-dining closures elsewhere.

Industry analysis on the closures has been measured: observers note that ingredient costs, rent, labor, and insurance all conspired against restaurants already operating on thin margins, in a consumer environment where spending is tightening. Chicago still has roughly 30 fully vegan restaurants, a number that would have seemed remarkable a decade ago. The vegan closure wave is real, but it's probably better understood as a sector shakeout than a verdict on demand.
